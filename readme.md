Abstract: This study focuses on the protection needs of airspace and key infrastructure such as power stations and airports, exploring methods to interfere with drone communication systems using electronic countermeasures. The core is the development of a low-cost, adaptable drone interference device based on software defined radio (SDR) technology. The study conducts interference technology experiments on three critical communication links of drones: positioning, radio control, and video transmission. The research also realizes the implementation of GPS deception technology, completing tests for static and dynamic deception modes. It was found that integrating dynamic deception technology with flight restrictions in restricted areas can effectively force drones to crash or exhibit abnormal flying behaviors. The study confirmed the effectiveness of interference and deception technologies in countering illegal drones, and pointed out the need for subsequent research on anti-drone systems against alternative communication bands such as 4G/5G to enhance the robustness of the countermeasure system in the evolving drone technology environment.  
Introduction: Drones have achieved rapid development and large-scale application in multiple fields including photography, surveying, logistics, etc. Despite the European Union's drone management regulations, illegal operations are still common, leading to numerous safety incidents. The current mainstream anti-drone systems detect and counter drones through electronic components such as radio signals, radar, optical tracking, etc. However, their integration into infrastructure construction faces multiple real challenges including regulations, costs, and technology; moreover, although interference technology has anti-drone effects, it may pose potential risks to other normal communications.
Based on the growing demand for practical and scalable anti-drone solutions in society, this study explores the actual effectiveness of radio frequency (RF) interference and deception technology as countermeasures against illegal drone operations. RF interference and deception can paralyze critical communication links such as drone command and control and GPS navigation, fundamentally limiting the operational capabilities of drones. This is of significant importance for enhancing the public safety protection levels of airports, public venues, and key infrastructure.
The core goal of this study is to evaluate the actual impact of interference technology on drone communication systems and design a cost-effective, adaptable drone interference device. The research findings provide valuable references for the future development of anti-drone technology and its integration into civilian environments; at the same time, it reveals the increasingly severe security challenges posed by advanced drones using alternative communication technologies such as 4G, and discusses the future direction of developing multi-frequency interference strategies.
Methodology: This study builds an experimental platform based on Linux (Ubuntu 24.04.1 LTS), focusing on hardware setup, software development, and multi-technology experimental design. All experiments are conducted on several commercial drones with weights≤250g and complying with regulatory restrictions, primarily in professional areas such as Portugal's St. Margaret's Military Camp.
1. Hardware System: The core consists of a signal generation and processing computer, ADALM-PLUTO SDR RF module, WYDZ-PA-1G-3GHz-1W power amplifier, and two dedicated antennas for 2.4GHz (WiFi) and 1.5GHz (GPS). These components address issues related to insufficient output power from SDR modules and compatibility issues with signal transmission.
2.
Software Tools: The core scripts were developed using the GNU Radio (3.10.1.1) open-source signal processing platform, optimized with VS Code for Python code, and GPS-SDR-SIM was used to generate GPS spoofing signals. The detection and validation of signals were completed using the Rohde & Schwarz FSV30 spectrum analyzer.
3  
Experimental Technology: Designing exclusive jamming/deception schemes for different communication links of drones. Firstly, for the GNSS navigation link, two types of interference methods are implemented: broadband blocking interference and BPSK protocol perceptive interference, as well as static (random position, no-fly zone) and dynamic (height change, no-fly zone + zero altitude) two types of GPS deception; secondly, for the 2.4GHz remote control and video link, a jamming script is designed to bypass SDR bandwidth restrictions through both frequency sweeping and random frequency selection methods, achieving full range interference coverage in this frequency band.
4  
Test Method: By adjusting the distance between the drone and the jamming device, or between the drone and the operator as the core variable, observe and record the communication status of the drone and changes in its flight behavior. This is used to evaluate the effectiveness and range of countermeasures against different jamming/deception technologies.
The rapid proliferation and technological upgrade of drones highlight the necessity for developing effective countermeasures. Especially as the communication methods of advanced drones become increasingly diverse, further complicating countermeasure efforts. The developed SDR-based drone interference system in this study, by integrating techniques such as blocking interference and GNSS-specific BPSK interference, precisely counteracts key communication links in drones' positioning, control, and video transmission. It demonstrates excellent anti-drone effectiveness across various practical scenarios. Its adaptability and targeted design are the core to achieving effective countermeasures against drones.
This study adopts the research method of indoor controlled fine tuning + outdoor field testing. In the real environment, through the two-step method of "interfering with the real GNSS signals first and then sending fake deceptive signals", it effectively neutralizes the interference of legitimate GNSS signals, verifying the robustness and scene adaptability of this countermeasure system.
The study has made several key conclusions: Firstly, obstruction interference can effectively paralyze the control and video transmission links of most drones, but the support for 4G backup communication by the Habson Zino Mini Pro drone reveals the limitations of single-band interference strategies, highlighting the urgent need for developing multi-band countermeasures. Secondly, compared to traditional blocking interference, BPSK interference targeting GNSS has advantages in terms of range and precision of interference, making it a more effective means to disable drone navigation systems. Thirdly, integrating dynamic deception technology that controls flight zones and heights with prohibited areas, can directly affect the flight dynamics of drones, being the most comprehensive and effective method to force drones to crash or produce abnormal flight behaviors.
This study provides a set of flexible and low-cost interference and deception solutions for the field of drone countermeasures, building a solid technical framework for airports, power stations, and other critical areas and infrastructure to prevent illegal drone activities. It also lays the foundation for further development of electronic warfare and anti-drone technologies.
For future research, this study proposes four directions: firstly, to develop multi-band interference systems to address the trend of drone reliance on 4G/5G communication technologies; secondly, to develop adaptive interference/deception systems that can respond to environmental changes and drone dynamics in real time, enhancing the flexibility of countermeasures; thirdly, to conduct specialized countermeasures against non-GPS global navigation systems such as Galileo and GLONASS, as well as drones equipped with AI-assisted map navigation; fourthly, to adopt SDR platforms with wider bandwidths and supporting amplifiers, antennas to break through the current equipment bandwidth limitations and comprehensively enhance the overall capabilities of the countermeasure system.
